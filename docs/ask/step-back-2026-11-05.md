# Ask step-back: where the bar actually is (2026-11-05)

A fresh look after Plan R2's verdict (`r2-findings-2026-11-04.md` §12). The question for the day: with E2B closed on the
model side (no adapter, no export) and retrieval's levers bounded, what would it take to reach 80% grounded / 90%
declined, and is E2B the wall or the passages?

Everything below is on the dev set (148 answerable + 61 unanswerable, `runs/dev-base-1` prompts), graded by the rubric.
Method: the recorded answer prompts are replayed through other readers (`answerers.py run … --backend cpu` on the
emulator for on-device models; Claude agents reading the same prompts for the ideal-reader ceiling), so only the reader
or the prompt changes and the passages stay what the phone chose. Noise on this set is about ±4 grounded items.

## 1. The ceiling: an ideal reader on the phone's passages

| Reader | Passages in the prompt | Grounded | Unanswerable declined | Run |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| E2B, Google's bundle (ships) | 4 | 83/148 (56%) | 51/61 (84%) | `dev-base-1` |
| Claude (ideal reader) | 4 | 105/148 (71%) | 57/61 (93%) | `dev-reader-claude-1` |
| Claude (ideal reader) | 12 (the ranker's top 12) | **118/148 (80%)** | 57/61 (93%) | `dev-reader12-claude-1` |

- With the 4 passages the app sends, **no reader passes**: every one of Claude's 27 answerable declines had no answer
  quote in the prompt, and its 16 misses are mostly two-part questions where the passages hold one half. The
  reader-side ceiling at 4 passages is about 105–111 of 148.
- With the same ranker's top 12 (`merge.py`'s PICK config over the recorded keyword and dense hits, no new search),
  the ideal reader reaches the bar exactly. Answer-quote coverage by pool size: 4 → 69, 8 → 79, 12 → 82, 16 → 87,
  24 → 93 of 148. The 13 gains came from every rank 5–12 (6 at rank 5, 5 at 8, 3 each at 6/9/10, 2 at 12, 1 at 11),
  24 of the 27 newly cited passages were encyclopedia articles with cosines 0.75–0.84, just under the top four's; 7
  were another chunk of a source already in the top 4; 6 of the 13 were multi-step questions that needed 4–5 passages at
  once. **No ranking rule picks them out**, which is why every reweighting in R2 failed: the lever is how much the
  reader sees, not the order.
- By domain the ideal reader at 12 clears 80% in medicine, maps, reference, science and technology; classics (31/44),
  food, making and thought stay under. Classics is retrieval-bound even for a perfect reader.

So: the bar is reachable from what the phone already finds, if the reader can use 12 candidates.

## 2. Can E2B use more candidates? No.

| E2B prompt | Grounded | Unanswerable declined | Run |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4 passages (ships) | 83 | 51/61 | `dev-base-1` |
| 3 passages | 80 | 51/61 | `dev-p3-1` |
| 8 passages (pool) | 82 | 45/61 | `dev-p8-1` |
| 12 passages (pool, text cut to 900 chars) | 80 | 50/61 | `dev-p12-1` |
| 12 passages trimmed to ~700 chars by embedder sentence cosine | 88 | 48/61 | `dev-p12t-1` |

E2B's grounded count is flat at 80–83 from 3 to 12 full passages (88 with 12 trimmed ones, +5, at the edge of noise); more candidates only make it answer more unanswerables
(8 passages: declines 51 → 45). The misses are the same kinds at every size: adjacent fact, misread number or entity,
arithmetic on multi-step items (which fail almost uniformly), half of a two-part question. The extra sources reach E2B
and it cannot convert them.

## 3. Prompt shape: nothing moves E2B

| Variant (`shape.py`) | Grounded | Unanswerable declined | Run |
|---|---|---|---|
| Question stated before the rules as well as after | 82 | 49/61 | `dev-qfirst-1` |
| Quote-first: copy the answering sentence verbatim, then answer | 77 | 31/61 | `dev-quote-1` |
| 3 passages | 80 | 51/61 | `dev-p3-1` |

Question-first is a null (7 gained, 7 lost). Quote-first is a clear loss: E2B copies real sentences well (159 of 168
quotes verbatim or near-verbatim in the cited passage, `shape.py verify`), but the format makes it bolder, not more
careful: it answered 30 of 61 unanswerables with a genuine quote that does not bear on the question, and its answers
were grounded less often. The verbatim check cannot catch an off-topic quote. With R2's rules-against-misreads result,
this closes the prompt family: E2B follows formats but its reading does not improve.

## 4. A bigger reader: E4B on the same prompts

| Reader | Passages | Grounded | Unanswerable declined | Grounded share of answers given | Run |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| E2B | 4 | 83 (56%) | 51/61 (84%) | 83/127 (65%) | `dev-base-1` |
| **E4B** | 4 | **93 (63%)** | **60/61 (98%)** | **93/112 (83%)** | `dev-e4b-1` |
| E4B | 8 | **96 (65%)** | 59/61 (97%) | 96/115 (83%) | `dev-e4b-p8-1` |
| E4B | 12 (text cut to 900 chars) | 84 (57%) | 56/61 (92%) | 84/115 (73%) | `dev-e4b-p12-1` |

Item by item, E4B turned 17 of E2B's wrong answers into declines and 11 into grounded answers, and lost 2. Its misses
are evasions and the same multi-step arithmetic. E4B is the precision profile the Preview needs (83% of what it says is
grounded, 98% declines) and is 10 points short of the grounded bar at 4 passages.

**The pool does not stack with E4B either.** 8 passages: 96 grounded (+3, inside noise; 5 declines became grounded, 4
wrong answers became declines, and 4 grounded answers were lost), declines 59/61, precision unchanged at 83%. 12
passages: 84 grounded (−9), precision 73%. Both Gemma readers lose focus past 8 passages; only the ideal reader turns
12 candidates into answers. The best on-device number of the day is E4B with 8 passages, **65% / 97%**.

Speed is the known cost (R4, 2026-10-26): on the Pixel 6 GPU E4B decodes 3.2 tok/s with a 20 s first-words wait; on
the emulator (M2 Max CPU) 23.7 tok/s, load 8 s. Newer phones run it at 20+ tok/s (the S26 Ultra at 22, R4 notes).

## 6. What this says

1. **E2B is the wall, not the passages.** Its grounded count does not move with prompt shape, passage count, or pool
   size; precision of its answers on answerable questions is 65%. The bar cannot be reached on E2B by any route tried in R2 or today.
2. **The passages can reach the bar** with a reader that can read 12 candidates (the ideal reader: 80% / 93%). The
   pool is the ranker's own top 12, available on the phone today at no extra search cost; the cost is prompt length
   (12 × 900 chars ≈ 3,300 tokens, about 7 s of prefill on the Pixel 6 at 451 tok/s, against 3 s now).
3. **E4B is a real step on the same prompts** (+10 grounded, +9 declines, precision 65 → 83%) and the only on-device
   reader measured that clears the decline bar; with 8 passages 65% / 97%. It does not gain from 12. Its speed rules
   it out on the Pixel 6, which is the minimum phone by Neal's call (R4). Even on a fast phone E4B sits 15 points under
   the grounded bar, so a bar-level Ask needs a reader between E4B and the ideal one (Gemma 4 12B class, desktop-only
   today), or the bar restated as precision (E4B: 83% of answers grounded, 97% declines).

So the decision ahead is about tiers and the bar, not tuning: Ask as it ships (E2B, 4 passages) stays a Preview on
every phone; phones that run E4B at speed could carry an E4B Ask (4–8 passages) at 63–65% grounded / 97–98% declined,
which passes a precision bar and fails the recall one. The Pixel 6 cannot have the bar-level Ask with any model that
exists today, and no phone can with the models that run on phones today.

## 8. The ceiling test: perfect targeting (2026-11-06)

What does a reader do when the passage holding the answer is certainly in its prompt? `tools/eval/ceiling.py` forces that
passage in (from the recorded hits for 94 of 148 items, from the answer's ZIM entry or EPUB chapter for 54) and fills the
rest from the app's own choice. It uses the dataset's answers, which no phone can know, so it is a ceiling and not a
method. Unanswerable items keep the app's passages, cut to the same size. Same rubric, blind graders, emulator CPU.

| Reader | Prompt | Grounded of 148 | Declined of 61 | Grounded of all answers given | Run |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| E2B | as ships, 4 | 83 (56%) | 51 | 83/137 | `dev-base-1` |
| E2B | real prompt, order reversed | 79 (53%) | 47 | 79/136 | `dev-rev-1` |
| E2B | answer only | 111 (75%) | 54 | 111/140 | `dev-c1-first-2` |
| E2B | answer first, 2 | 107 (72%) | 53 | 107/143 | `dev-c2-first-2` |
| E2B | answer first, 4 | 105 (70%) | 49 | 105/150 | `dev-c4-first-2` |
| E2B | answer last, 4 | 113 (76%) | 49 | 113/153 | `dev-c4-last-2` |
| E4B | as ships, 4 | 93 (62%) | 60 | 93/113 | `dev-e4b-1` |
| **E4B** | **answer last, 4** | **125 (84%)** | **60** | **125/139 (90%)** | `dev-e4b-c4-last-1` |

The last column counts wrong answers to unanswerable questions, so its E2B figures are lower than the answerable-only
precision in §4 (83/127).

1. **Passages were the larger part of the gap.** Perfect targeting adds 22–30 grounded on E2B and 32 on E4B (33 gained,
   1 lost). §6's "E2B is the wall, not the passages" is too strong: more passages did not help, better ones do.
2. **E2B still misses the bar with perfect passages:** 76% at best, and 49–54 of 61 declines against the 90% bar.
3. **E4B clears both bars with perfect passages:** 84% / 98%. Allowing a margin of none, a real retriever must recover 25 of
   the 32: it can lose only 7.
4. **Order is no shippable lever.** Answer-last beat answer-first by 8 on E2B (16 gained, 8 lost), but reversing the real
   prompts lost 4 (4 gained, 8 lost), with declines down 4. E4B was tested answer-last only, so its answer-first number is unknown.
5. Remaining E4B misses: 11 of 23 classics, 11 multi-step (10 of 21 grounded, no worked sums in these prompts).

Caveats: dev set, used heavily already, noise about ±4; forced passages favour a reader beyond what any retriever will do,
and text from packs is a window around the quote, not the app's chunk. The first round left footnote marks in the
pack text, which E2B copied as bad citations; `-2` runs replay those 54 items with clean text (`-1` runs are superseded).

**What this changes:** the route to the bar is now E4B plus better targeting, which leaves the Pixel 6 out unless E4B speeds
up there. Next: E4B speed on the Pixel 7 (§7), and a pilot of the question index, measured first offline by whether the
answer's chunk reaches the top 4, then on the held-out set.

## 9. The question index: no significant gain (2026-11-07)

A local model (Qwen3.6-35B-A3B, `llama-server`) wrote three everyday questions for each of the 39,673 chunks the dev
questions draw on, rival chunks included. Each was scored offline by whether the answer's literal quote reaches the ranked
top 4 / 8 / 12 (`tools/vectors/qindex.py score dev-base-1`, same int8 vectors and `merge.py` ranker as the app). "Depth" is
how many chunks per question had questions written: 25 means only the ones near the answer, "all" means every chunk.

| Variant | Depth | @4 | @8 | @12 | In top 24 | Unanswerable passing the gate (of 61) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Text only (today) | — | 51 | 61 | 64 | 86 | 46 |
| A: questions as extra vectors, passage form | 25 | 59 | 71 | 80 | 99 | 49 |
| A | all | 53 | 68 | 77 | 91 | 50 |
| A2: questions as extra vectors, query form | 25 | 65 | 77 | 83 | 102 | 60 |
| **A2** | **all** | **56** | 69 | 75 | 87 | **61** |
| B: questions inside the chunk's one vector | all | 55 | 62 | 69 | 89 | 42 |
| C: chunk vector averaged with its questions | all | 52 | 66 | 74 | 88 | 54 |

1. **The depth-25 gain was rigging.** With questions only on chunks near the answer, A2 reached 65 at 4; once every chunk had
   them, rivals rose too and it fell to 56 (+17 gained, −12 lost against text only).
2. **The best variant breaks declines:** A2 passes all 61 unanswerable questions through the gate (46 today).
3. **Far short of what E4B needs:** §8 puts that at about 125 of 148 at 4. The misses are not wording: 23 answers are not
   indexed at all (the 12-chunk cap per article and infobox text left out), and 40 more rank below 24.
4. A and A2 also cost the phone about four times the vectors; B and C cost nothing but gain less.

**What this changes:** retrieval tuning has not gained significant ground, so the next Ask R&D session changes how E2B is
asked rather than what it is given. Neal's principles for small models: small input, one specific operation, a strictly
defined output format, output that can be checked automatically. A starting point to test: one passage per call; the
model answers `NO`, or `YES` plus the sentence that answers, copied verbatim; code checks the copy is verbatim and builds
the reply and any sums; no valid sentence from any passage means a decline. Each step can be scored without graders.
The open cost is speed: up to four short calls instead of one long one.

## 10. Small steps (Plan S1, 2026-11-07): no gain

Plan S1 (`docs/superpowers/plans/2026-11-07-plan-s1-small-steps-ask.md`) applied §9's principles. `tools/eval/point.py`
built each step from `dev-base-1`'s recorded passages. Runs used E2B on the emulator's CPU, with blind Fable graders and the
same rubric.

- **Point:** the question and one passage split into numbered sentences (~370 tokens). Reply `0` or a sentence number.
- **Choose:** the pointed sentences listed with their titles (~200 tokens). Reply one number or `0`.
- **Then one of:** quote the chosen sentence (code only); **Say**, a one-sentence reword with a word check (~19 tokens out);
  or today's answer prompt with only the chosen passage.

| Run | Reader | Grounded of 148 | Declined of 61 | Grounded of answers given | Multi-step |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `dev-base-1` | E2B, 4 passages, one prompt | 83 | 51 | 83/137 (61%) | 3/21 |
| `s1-q4` | Point → Choose → quote | 66 | 50 | 66/140 | 0/21 |
| `s1-s4` | Point → Choose → Say | 66 | 50 | 66/140 | 1/21 |
| `s1-o4` | Point → Choose → one-passage answer | 74 | 53 | 74/116 (64%) | 0/21 |

1. **E2B keeps a strict format.** Point had 1 off-format reply in 692 calls, and Say's word check passed 111 of 140. The rules
   work for format; the trouble is judgement.
2. **Point can't tell which passage lacks the answer.** It names a sentence in 82% of passages on answerable questions and 33%
   on unanswerable ones, so the first passage that points is usually wrong. Choose does better with the app's 4 than with
   the ranker's 8, because more candidates confuse it.
3. **One sentence is too narrow an answer.** The graders' main failures were a quote that never names the subject, a second
   fact left out (multi-step 0/21), and a wrong pick. Rewording (Say) fixed none of these.
4. **One chosen passage gains no ground.** It declines a little more (53, +2) and is a little more precise (64% against 61%),
   both within noise. It loses 9 grounded, mostly by declining answerable questions (35 against 20). §8's 111 with the
   answer passage alone needed perfect targeting, and Choose's pick is far from that.

**What this changes:** small steps don't give E2B the judgement it lacks. Ask stays as it ships on the Pixel 6. Not tried:
two chosen passages for multi-step questions, and the chosen sentence with its neighbours. Neither addresses the wrong
picks, which are the biggest loss.
