# Ask step-back: where the bar actually is (2026-11-05)

A fresh look after Plan R2's verdict (`r2-findings-2026-11-04.md` §12). The question for the day: with E2B closed on the
model side (no adapter, no export) and retrieval's levers bounded, what would it take to reach 80% grounded / 90%
declined, and is E2B the wall or the passages?

Everything below is on the dev set (148 answerable + 61 unanswerable, `runs/dev-base-1` prompts), graded by the rubric.
Method: the recorded answer prompts are replayed through other readers (`answerers.py run … --backend cpu` on the
emulator for on-device models; Claude agents reading the same prompts for the ideal-reader ceiling), so only the reader
or the prompt changes and the passages stay what the phone chose. Noise on this set is about ±4 grounded items.

## 1. The ceiling: an ideal reader on the phone's passages

| Reader | Passages in the prompt | Grounded | Unanswerable declined | Run |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| E2B, Google's bundle (ships) | 4 | 83/148 (56%) | 51/61 (84%) | `dev-base-1` |
| Claude (ideal reader) | 4 | 105/148 (71%) | 57/61 (93%) | `dev-reader-claude-1` |
| Claude (ideal reader) | 12 (the ranker's top 12) | **118/148 (80%)** | 57/61 (93%) | `dev-reader12-claude-1` |

- With the 4 passages the app sends, **no reader passes**: every one of Claude's 27 answerable declines had no answer
  quote in the prompt, and its 16 misses are mostly two-part questions where the passages hold one half. The
  reader-side ceiling at 4 passages is about 105–111 of 148.
- With the same ranker's top 12 (`merge.py`'s PICK config over the recorded keyword and dense hits, no new search),
  the ideal reader reaches the bar exactly. Answer-quote coverage by pool size: 4 → 69, 8 → 79, 12 → 82, 16 → 87,
  24 → 93 of 148. The 13 gains came from every rank 5–12 (6 at rank 5, 5 at 8, 3 each at 6/9/10, 2 at 12, 1 at 11),
  24 of the 27 newly cited passages were encyclopedia articles with cosines 0.75–0.84, just under the top four's; 7
  were another chunk of a source already in the top 4; 6 of the 13 were multi-step questions that needed 4–5 passages at
  once. **No ranking rule picks them out**, which is why every reweighting in R2 failed: the lever is how much the
  reader sees, not the order.
- By domain the ideal reader at 12 clears 80% in medicine, maps, reference, science and technology; classics (31/44),
  food, making and thought stay under. Classics is retrieval-bound even for a perfect reader.

So: the bar is reachable from what the phone already finds, if the reader can use 12 candidates.

## 2. Can E2B use more candidates? No.

| E2B prompt | Grounded | Unanswerable declined | Run |
|---|---|---|---|
| 4 passages (ships) | 83 | 51/61 | `dev-base-1` |
| 3 passages | 80 | 51/61 | `dev-p3-1` |
| 8 passages (pool) | 82 | 45/61 | `dev-p8-1` |
| 12 passages (pool, text cut to 900 chars) | 80 | 50/61 | `dev-p12-1` |
| 12 passages trimmed to ~700 chars by embedder sentence cosine | 88 | 48/61 | `dev-p12t-1` |

E2B's grounded count is flat at 80–83 from 3 to 12 full passages (88 with 12 trimmed ones, +5, at the edge of noise); more candidates only make it answer more unanswerables
(8 passages: declines 51 → 45). The misses are the same kinds at every size: adjacent fact, misread number or entity,
arithmetic on multi-step items (which fail almost uniformly), half of a two-part question. The extra sources reach E2B
and it cannot convert them.

## 3. Prompt shape: nothing moves E2B

| Variant (`shape.py`) | Grounded | Unanswerable declined | Run |
|---|---|---|---|
| Question stated before the rules as well as after | 82 | 49/61 | `dev-qfirst-1` |
| Quote-first: copy the answering sentence verbatim, then answer | 77 | 31/61 | `dev-quote-1` |
| 3 passages | 80 | 51/61 | `dev-p3-1` |

Question-first is a null (7 gained, 7 lost). Quote-first is a clear loss: E2B copies real sentences well (159 of 168
quotes verbatim or near-verbatim in the cited passage, `shape.py verify`), but the format makes it bolder, not more
careful: it answered 30 of 61 unanswerables with a genuine quote that does not bear on the question, and its answers
were grounded less often. The verbatim check cannot catch an off-topic quote. With R2's rules-against-misreads result,
this closes the prompt family: E2B follows formats but its reading does not improve.

## 4. A bigger reader: E4B on the same prompts

| Reader | Passages | Grounded | Unanswerable declined | Grounded share of answers given | Run |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| E2B | 4 | 83 (56%) | 51/61 (84%) | 83/137 (61%) | `dev-base-1` |
| **E4B** | 4 | **93 (63%)** | **60/61 (98%)** | **93/112 (83%)** | `dev-e4b-1` |
| E4B | 8 | **96 (65%)** | 59/61 (97%) | 96/115 (83%) | `dev-e4b-p8-1` |
| E4B | 12 (text cut to 900 chars) | 84 (57%) | 56/61 (92%) | 84/115 (73%) | `dev-e4b-p12-1` |

Item by item, E4B turned 17 of E2B's wrong answers into declines and 11 into grounded answers, and lost 2. Its misses
are evasions and the same multi-step arithmetic. E4B is the precision profile the Preview needs (83% of what it says is
grounded, 98% declines) and is 10 points short of the grounded bar at 4 passages.

**The pool does not stack with E4B either.** 8 passages: 96 grounded (+3, inside noise; 5 declines became grounded, 4
wrong answers became declines, and 4 grounded answers were lost), declines 59/61, precision unchanged at 83%. 12
passages: 84 grounded (−9), precision 73%. Both Gemma readers lose focus past 8 passages; only the ideal reader turns
12 candidates into answers. The best on-device number of the day is E4B with 8 passages, **65% / 97%**.

Speed is the known cost (R4, 2026-10-26): on the Pixel 6 GPU E4B decodes 3.2 tok/s with a 20 s first-words wait; on
the emulator (M2 Max CPU) 23.7 tok/s, load 8 s. Newer phones run it at 20+ tok/s (the S26 Ultra at 22, R4 notes).

## 6. What this says

1. **E2B is the wall, not the passages.** Its grounded count does not move with prompt shape, passage count, or pool
   size; precision of its answers is 61%. The bar cannot be reached on E2B by any route tried in R2 or today.
2. **The passages can reach the bar** with a reader that can read 12 candidates (the ideal reader: 80% / 93%). The
   pool is the ranker's own top 12, available on the phone today at no extra search cost; the cost is prompt length
   (12 × 900 chars ≈ 3,300 tokens, about 7 s of prefill on the Pixel 6 at 451 tok/s, against 3 s now).
3. **E4B is a real step on the same prompts** (+10 grounded, +9 declines, precision 61 → 83%) and the only on-device
   reader measured that clears the decline bar; with 8 passages 65% / 97%. It does not gain from 12. Its speed rules
   it out on the Pixel 6, which is the minimum phone by Neal's call (R4). Even on a fast phone E4B sits 15 points under
   the grounded bar, so a bar-level Ask needs a reader between E4B and the ideal one (Gemma 4 12B class, desktop-only
   today), or the bar restated as precision (E4B: 83% of answers grounded, 97% declines).

So the decision ahead is about tiers and the bar, not tuning: Ask as it ships (E2B, 4 passages) stays a Preview on
every phone; phones that run E4B at speed could carry an E4B Ask (4–8 passages) at 63–65% grounded / 97–98% declined,
which passes a precision bar and fails the recall one. The Pixel 6 cannot have the bar-level Ask with any model that
exists today, and no phone can with the models that run on phones today.

   wrong-chunk-of-the-right-book and plot questions without a title, which a reader's focus does not fix. The domains
   where E2B is already near the bar are maps, medicine and reference (75% each at 4 passages, 83–91% with the trimmed

   reference shelf" (encyclopedia, medicine, maps: one collection family, its own prompt, the trimmed-12 pool) rather
   than as a Classics expert; the Classics question needs the notes/cards and the within-book second look (R2 §4.2)
   before any reader can pass it. A per-collection prompt is cheap to replay the same way as today's variants.
