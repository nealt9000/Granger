# Ask against its bar: findings as of 2026-11-04

Written at the end of the day the development set was first scored, as the starting point for a fresh brainstorming
session. The numbers come from Plan R2 (`docs/superpowers/plans/2026-11-02-plan-r2-pass-or-stop.md`, Tasks 10–11),
the blind by-kind report (`docs/ask/r2-heldout-1-by-kind.md`) and the day's runs. Everything here is on branch
`r2-pass-or-stop`.

## 2. What the misses are

**Dev (69 misses of 158), with grades:**

| Kind | n | Share |
|---|---|---|
| Answer in the prompt, reply not grounded (wrong passage cited, a number misread, a two-part question half answered, an adjacent fact) | 25 | 36% |
| Answer in the prompt, model declined | 7 | 10% |
| Declined by the ranking gate before the model ran | 8 | 12% |
| Found by a search, not among the 4 | 18 | 26% |
| Never found by either search | 11 | 16% |

So on dev the model owns 46% of the misses and retrieval 54%. Classics: 10 of its 25 misses had the answer in the
prompt. On held-out (blind, by kind) retrieval was 25 of 42 and the model 17 of 42.

**Inside retrieval, by kind** (dev; held-out and tuning agree):
- *Found, not chosen* is the big one, not *never found*: counting a hit anywhere in either search's list, only 15
  of 148 dev answers are never found; 43 are found and not chosen. Classics carries 20 of those 43.
- *The right source, the wrong chunk*: in 27 dev misses (19 tuning, 30 held-out) the right book or article was among
  the 4 but another chunk of it. All 79 such chunks came from the dense list, across 67 different chapters, and the
  answer's chunk scores **lower** lexically than the chosen ones in 15 of 16: the other chapters use the question's
  words, the answer's chapter doesn't. No reweighting fixes that.
- *Wikipedia about the work beats the work*: in the 20 classics found-not-chosen misses, `wikipedia_en_top` filled
  36 of the 80 slots. Scripture questions ("who got swallowed by a giant fish") got four encyclopedia passages.
- *Fiction without a cue word*: questions whose answer is in a novel, with no literary word in them ("novel",
  "character", "wrote"…), missed 13 of 20 on dev and 8 of 9 on held-out; with a cue word 4 of 9 and 4 of 8.
- *Plot descriptions with no title* ("the story with the headless horseman"): 5 of 42 held-out misses, several on
  dev; the keywords become plot nouns that match unrelated articles.
- *The Classics notes* (one-page cards for the 50 most-read works, built for exactly these questions) are found and
  not chosen: among the hits for 13 of 31 held-out classics questions and chosen for 2; on dev a note for the right
  work was among the hits for 7 of 26 classics misses, holding the answer's words for 5. The dev set deliberately
  spans 42 books, mostly outside the 50.
- *Spanish passages on English questions*: took a slot on 17 of 202 tuning, 10 of 171 held-out, 11 of 209 dev
  questions. Fixed today (`ResearchPipeline` drops passages whose language isn't the question's).
- *The gate* declines 8 dev answerables before the model (6 with the passage found by a search), but lowering it
  recovers none of them by replay (their passage isn't in the top 4 ungated either) and costs 11 declines. It also
  does most of the declining: 56 of tuning's 71 declines, 26 of held-out's 44, 25 of dev's 51 come from it.

**Multi-step**: 5 of 19 held-out, 13 of 20 tuning (after the date work). With both facts in the prompt the model
often declines ("the passages don't give the computed value") or skips the arithmetic.

## 4. What's left, by kind

Retrieval (bounded, cheap, by kind):
1. **A reserved slot for a Classics note of the named work** when one is among the hits (mirror `Entities.reserve`).
   Ceiling: about 5 of 26 classics misses on dev, 8 of 14 found-not-chosen classics on held-out.
2. **A second look inside the found source**: when the 4 concentrate on one book or article, search within it for
   the answer's chunk by a different route than the question's words (the dense neighbours of the chosen chunks, the
   rewrite's alternates, or the chapter whose heading matches). Addresses the 27-per-set "right source, wrong chunk"
   kind; costs a search step, not a weight.
3. **Plot descriptions with no title**: route them to the notes and to Wikipedia's article about the work, or ask the
   model for the title first (one short extra turn).

Model (the half retrieval can't reach):
4. **A self-check pass**: after the answer, the model checks each cited claim against its passage and withdraws or
   corrects; replayable over recorded prompts on the emulator (`answerers.py`), a few seconds a turn on the phone.
5. **Multi-step arithmetic in code** beyond dates (counts, ages, differences), since the model won't do it.
6. **The LoRA**, when LiteRT-LM loads adapters on the GPU bundle, or a merged model if its halved decode speed is
   acceptable on big phones only.
7. **A larger model on bigger phones** (the E4B catalog entry is still there, `evalPassed: false`).

Not levers: more passages (tried), reweighting (tried five ways), lowering the gate (tried).

## 5. How we work now

- **Three sets, three roles.** Tuning: regression only. Dev (`dataset/dev.json`, blind-written, 219 items): where
  every change is judged, by kind, never item by item; scored as often as needed. Held-out: scored once more, on the
  phone, only with changes that held on dev.
- **The emulator.** An arm64 AVD on the X10 Pro SSD runs the debug build with E2B's CPU bundle: the tuning split in
  20 min, the dev set in 25, first words 6 s. Parity with the phone: same outcome on 197 of 202, 80% passage overlap,
  grounded 102 vs 101; the keyword rewrite differs on about half the questions (CPU vs GPU numerics). Deltas come
  from the emulator; the pass-or-stop number from the phone. `~/.cache/granger/emu-stage/start-emulator.sh`,
  `load-library.sh`; `ANDROID_SERIAL=emulator-5580`; `eval.py run … --passages N` exists now.
- **Grading** is a Fable agent per run (about 5 min, ~350k tokens for 190 replies); the blind grader's by-kind report
  is the model for reading the held-out set without seeing it.
- **Noise** is about ±4 answered items per set; a lever has to clear it on dev before it's believed.

## 7. The evening's brainstorm, checked (2026-11-04)

**The LoRA blocker is platform-wide and smaller than logged.** E2B runs through LiteRT-LM's GPU delegate on every
Android GPU; the adapter failure is in that delegate and in the published bundles, not in Pixel hardware. PR #3497
("Fix Gemma-4 GPU LoRA support for quantized models in MLDrift delegate", one open-source `.cc` file) merged 2026-10-08
and is in LiteRT-LM v0.19.0 (2026-11-02; `gradle/libs.versions.toml` pins 0.18.0). Still missing for a runtime adapter:
a bundle exported with LoRA inputs (`litert-torch` 0.10.0's `export_hf` has no LoRA option at all; the published bundles
report `supported_lora_ranks: []`) and a Kotlin setting for supported ranks (0.19.0's `EngineConfig` has none;
`LoraConfig` is still only `loraPath`/`audioLoraPath`; the C and Python APIs have the setter). So a retry of a sidecar
adapter on 0.19.0 is not possible yet without converter work upstream. The 0.19.0 bump was not taken today: it changes
GPU sampling ("matches CPU output for the same seed") and would confound the sets.

**The merged-model route works and its cost is the export, not the adapter.** Runs `lora-*` (speed-10, Pixel 6 GPU):
Google's bundle decodes 14.5 tok/s; our `export_hf` of stock E2B with no LoRA 9.1; with rope/swiglu composites 9.6;
the merged toy adapter 8.1. First words unchanged (~3 s model-side). Closing that gap is an export-recipe question
(`~/granger-models/lora/sweep.sh`), independent of #3173. Reverse-engineering the runtime is not the route: it is
Apache-2.0 source, and the fix that mattered landed in the open.

**A different, lighter model to tune ourselves** re-runs R4's speed screen, where every other 2–3B model was over 12 s
to first words or killed for memory; E2B's 451 tok/s prefill is four times the next best. A 1B-class model on the CPU
reads a 6,000-character prompt more slowly and worse, and a LoRA fixes behaviour, not reading. E2B stays.

**Per-collection quotas don't help; one reserved place for the work does, in retrieval only.** Replayed over the recorded
hits (`merge.py`, which agrees with the device on 199 of 209 dev questions), answer-in-the-4 by location:

| Ranker | Dev (148) | Dev gained/lost | Dev classics (44) | Tuning emulator / phone gained/lost |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Shipped hybrid | 84 | | 18 | |
| Cap 2 per collection | 85 | +6 / −7 | 20 | +3/−1, +1/−5 |
| Round-robin by collection | 75 | +7 / −17 | 17 | +2/−16, +2/−16 |
| Reserve 1 place for a book hit | 89 | +5 / −0 | 23 | +1/−0, +2/−2 |
| Same, book hit at cosine ≥ 0.78 only | 87 | +3 / −0 | 21 | +2/−0, +2/−0 |

Spreading places across collections costs maps, medicine and reference, where the dominant collection is the right one;
the asymmetry is the specific one already logged (the encyclopedia article about a work beats the work). The confident
book reserve is in the code (`Ranking.reserveBook`, `ResearchPipeline(reserveBook)`, `eval.py run --books on`,
`merge.py … book1 c0.78`), off in the app.

**Run `dev-books-1`** (emulator, baseline flags + `--books on`): recall +3/−0, exactly the replay's three classics
questions; a book chunk newly reached the prompt on 9 questions. **Grounded 83 → 84, declined 51 → 51.** The model
cited the new book passage on 0 of the 7 it answered: two of the three gains were already grounded from the encyclopedia
passages and the third stayed a decline; the +1 is an unrelated multi-step flip (one gained, one lost). By kind: a

**Where that leaves the order:** retrieval's cheap by-kind levers are now spent except the second look inside the found
source (§4.2) and plot-descriptions-without-a-title (§4.3). The model half needs the merged-model route (behaviour
LoRA merged and re-exported; the export recipe gap is the cost) or waiting for the converter to export LoRA inputs.

## 8. The export gap is not a recipe (2026-11-04, evening)

Checked the headers of Google's two bundles (`~/.cache/granger/emu-stage/models*/`). The CPU bundle
(`gemma-4-E2B-it.litertlm`, 2.59 GB) is a TFLite bundle: `tf_lite_prefill_decode`, per-layer embedder, embedder,
`prefer_activation_type fp16`, plus a `tf_lite_mtp_drafter` (a speculative-decoding drafter). The GPU bundle
(`gemma-4-E2B-it-gpu.litertlm`, 2.0 GB, created 2026-08-06) holds **no TFLite graph**: one section of model type
`tf_lite_artisan_text_decoder`, `backend_constraint gpu_artisan`, `gpu_artisan_weights_version 0.0`. Google's 14.5 tok/s
comes from a hand-built GPU decoder ("artisan") reading a weights-only section; our `export_hf` produces a TFLite
`tf_lite_prefill_decode` graph that runs through the generic GPU delegate (9.1 tok/s; 1,464 MB of graphics memory
against 1,210). No `export_hf` flag turns one into the other, so the §3 sweep (ring buffer, composites, mixed
precision) was never going to close the gap. The open question for the merged route is whether any public tool writes
a `gpu_artisan` weights section from a merged checkpoint; a web check of that was started and not finished.

What the slower decode costs, from the speed-10 runs (64 tokens, Pixel 6 GPU): model total 6.9 s (Google) vs 9.6 s
(ours), first words the same (~3 s model-side, 12 s send to first words). About +2.7 s per 64 reply tokens, so a
typical Ask reply costs roughly 3–5 s more in total on the Pixel 6; acceptable as a Preview, not as the release model
on that phone. Bigger GPUs were not measured.

State of the toolchain: the HF weights are on the Mac (the hub's shared blob store, 10.2 GB); the export venv and the
exported bundles were gone. Rebuilt 2026-11-04 on the X10 Pro SSD: `/Volumes/X10 Pro/granger-models/litert-venv`
(Python 3.11, litert-torch 0.10.0, torch 2.14, transformers 5.22, PEFT 0.21; `setup.sh` there), since the Mac has 59 GB
free and the SSD 1.3 TB. `export_hf` also needs `tensorflow` installed, which `litert-torch` doesn't pull in.

**Upstream, checked 2026-11-04 (web, report only; nothing bumped).** LiteRT-LM's newest release is still v0.19.0
(Google Maven agrees: `litertlm-android` latest 0.19.0, uploaded 2026-10-29). The Kotlin `EngineConfig` on main has no
supported-LoRA-ranks field and the JNI never calls `SetSupportedLoraRanks`; C, Python, Swift and JS all have it.
litert-torch's newest is 0.10.0 (2026-10-20); `export_hf` on main has no LoRA kwarg among ~90; the only LoRA path is the
legacy per-model `convert_to_tflite.py` route (`lora_ranks`), which has no Gemma 4 script. Tracking issue litert-torch
#1321 (2026-10-09) is open with no comments. #3173: no maintainer action since the 2026-08-10 assignment.

The artisan path: `gpu_artisan` is LiteRT-LM's "GPU hand-written path backend" (`Backend::GPU_ARTISAN`,
`TF_LITE_ARTISAN_TEXT_DECODER`); the engine switches to it when the bundle has that section. Its executor is **not in the
repo** (build files call it the legacy artisan executor, excluded from the open build; issue #3512 asked for the source
and got none); it ships only inside Google's prebuilt binaries. The weights section is the MediaPipe LLM Inference
".bin" format (`odml.infra.proto.LlmParameters`); the `-gpu` and `-web` bundles are byte-for-byte the same size and the
HF model card never mentions how the `-gpu` file was built. **No public tool writes a gpu_artisan weights section**
(litert-torch 0.10.0 wheel, litert-lm-api 0.19.0 wheel, `litertlm_builder`, MediaPipe: zero hits). Two more facts for
later: the artisan path is where runtime LoRA works by design (`GpuArtisanConfig.supported_lora_ranks` is the only
setting `SetSupportedLoraRanks` reaches, and the -gpu bundle gets as far as `LlmOpenCl::LoadLoRA` before rejecting rank
16), and PR #3497 fixed the *other* path (TFLite graphs on the MLDrift compiled model). So the stock GPU bundle needs a
Kotlin/JNI setter (the C API has it; our own shim could call it) **and** a LoRA file in the artisan `LlmParameters`
format, which nothing public writes. The merged route stays the only one open, at the TFLite path's 9–10 tok/s.

Recipe detail worth one export: litert-torch main's Gemma 4 defaults are `dynamic_wi4b32_afp32` (block-32 int4),
`apply_gpu_composites`, `externalize_weights`, prefill lengths 128/1024, against our `dynamic_wi4_afp32`, prefill 256.
Not a route to 14.5, but the merged export should use them if 0.10.0 has them.

**Step 1's answer:** the 9 vs 14.5 tok/s gap is a different executor, not our recipe; the ~10% target is unreachable
from `export_hf`. The slower decode costs about 2.7 s per 64 reply tokens on the Pixel 6 (first words unchanged):
acceptable for a Preview and on bigger phones, not for the release model on the Pixel 6.

## 10. Step 3: the merged route, measured (2026-11-04, night)

**Method.** The model half is judged on the model alone: `answerers.py run dev-base-1 NEW --model … --backend cpu` on
the emulator replays the 209 recorded dev answer prompts (same passages, same order) with another bundle, and the
replies are graded by the rubric. Three bundles: Google's CPU bundle (dev-base-1 itself), our export of **stock** E2B
(`stock-b32comp`: `export_hf`, `dynamic_wi4b32_afp32`, GPU composites, the control that separates the export's cost from
the adapter's), and the **merged** LoRA exported the same way.

**The export alone changes the model's behaviour, before any adapter.** Run `dev-stock-b32comp` (emulator CPU, 6.3 s load,
5.5 s first words, 20 tok/s decode): on the 148 answerable prompts, 38 that Google's bundle answered now get
`NOT IN LIBRARY` (answered → declined 38, declined → answered 0; answerable declines 20 → 58); on the 61 unanswerable,
declined 51 → 58 (9 answered → declined, 2 the other way). The flips span every domain (classics 11, making 6, medicine
5, science 4). So our post-training int4 of the bf16 checkpoint is a more timid model than Google's QAT bundle, and the
merged route pays that before the LoRA pays anything back. Graded: **grounded 71/148 (48%) against 83 (56%); declined
58/61 (95%) against 51 (84%)**. Of the 88 answers it gave, 71 were grounded (81%, the same rate as the baseline's
answers), so the loss is all in the extra declines: the export made the model timid, not sloppy. Its 17 not-grounded
replies are the familiar kinds: evades the question 6, adjacent fact 5, misread entity or number 3, half of a two-part
question 3; multi-step 2/21. All 19 medical answers stayed inside the passages.

So on the dev set the merged route starts 12 grounded answers behind Google's bundle, and the adapter has to earn those
back before it earns anything. Whether the timidity is the block-32 recipe, int4 itself, or the CPU path is a one-export
question each (channel-wise `dynamic_wi4_afp32`, then int8) and is queued behind the merged export for memory's sake.

**Memory, for the record.** A training step on MPS without care was a 60 GB footprint (full-vocabulary logits for every
prompt token in fp32, plus saved activations) and swamped the Mac. Reply-only logits bring a 2,088-token step to 30 GB
peak and 5.4 s; gradient checkpointing to 13.6 GB and 7.7 s but without the K/V cache (`tools/lora/memprobe.py`). Run
`lora-r16-1`: val loss before training 0.745 (tuning pairs).

**Training (run `lora-r16-1`).** 450 pairs, 2 epochs, 225 optimizer steps of 4, 58 min on MPS at a 21 GB footprint
once the allocator cache is released each step. Val loss (49 held-back tuning pairs) 0.745 → 0.272 → 0.262. The merged
checkpoint (10.2 GB bf16) exported in 4.5 min with the control's recipe (`lora-r16-1`, 2.84 GB) and again channel-wise
(`lora-r16-1-c4`, 2.55 GB), see next.

**Block-32 int4 does not run on the phone GPU.** `stock-b32comp` on the Pixel 6 failed at load, `Failed to modify graph
with delegate` (litert_compiled_model.h:1961, LiteRT-LM 0.18.0): the MLDrift delegate rejects the block-wise quantized
graph. It runs on the CPU (the emulator replays above). So the shipping recipe is the channel-wise `dynamic_wi4_afp32`
of the 2026-11-02 sweep (plus GPU composites), and the control that matters is `stock-c4comp`; the block-32 replays
stand as the CPU behaviour comparison at equal recipe. Speed-10 on the phone: `stock-c4comp` then `lora-r16-1-c4`.

**The merged model `lora-r16-1` on the dev prompts (emulator, block-32 bundle, same recipe as its control): it learned to
decline.** Answerable declines 98/148 (the stock export 58, Google's bundle 20), unanswerable declined 61/61. Against
the stock export: 38 answered → declined, 0 the other way; against Google's bundle 77 → declined. It answered 50: **grounded 44/148
(30%)**, 6 not grounded (the answers it gives are grounded at 88%, against 81% for both other bundles; the misses are
multi-step fusions and half-answers). Median reply 4 tokens, which is the decline token. Where it did answer with a book chunk among the passages it cited the
book 7 of 9 times (Google's bundle 17 of 26, the stock export 12 of 20), too few to call.

By kind: the training mix was 31% decline targets (54 recorded, 100 swapped-passage) and the 306 grounded targets were
E2B's own replies, so the declines were the only new signal in the gradient, on top of an export that already tilts
toward the decline token. Second run `lora-r16-2`: 414 pairs, 30 declines (7%), the 39 authored answers twice.

**Composites, not block size, break the phone load.** The channel-wise `stock-c4comp` and `lora-r16-1-c4` bundles fail on
the Pixel 6 GPU the same way as block-32 (`Failed to modify graph with delegate`). The one flag today's exports added over
the 2026-11-02 sweep is `--apply_gpu_composites true` (the master switch; it implies the fused SDPA composite), so that
is the suspect; `export.sh` is back to the 11-02 rope+swiglu recipe and `lora-r16-1-rs` / `stock-rs` are exporting for
the phone.

**The merged model runs on the Pixel 6 GPU at the 11-02 recipe** (`lora-r16-1-rs`, speed-10, 64 tokens): decode 8.8
tok/s (stock at the same recipe 9.6, Google 14.5), first words 3.3 s (3.1, 3.0), 10.1 s for 64 tokens (9.3, 6.9),
graphics 1,388 MB (1,388, 1,210), load 58 s (29, 26). So the composite master switch was the load failure, the
adapter costs under 1 tok/s on top of the export's cost, and the phone number for a merged model stays what §8 said.
Today's toolchain reproduces the 11-02 export: `stock-rs` decodes 9.7 tok/s (p90 9.4), first words 3.0 s; the second
merged model `lora-r16-2-rs` 9.5 tok/s (p90 8.3), first words 3.2 s, 9.5 s for 64 tokens. The adapter is free on speed.

**The quantization recipe sets the decline rate.** Run `dev-stock-rs` (stock E2B, channel-wise `dynamic_wi4_afp32`,
rope+swiglu composites, the phone recipe) on the same 209 dev prompts: answerable declines **11** (block-32 export 58,
Google 20), unanswerable declined **35/61** (58, 51). Same checkpoint, same prompts, same CPU: block-32 int4 makes E2B
timid and channel-wise int4 makes it bold, with Google's QAT bundle in between. The decision at the decline token is
that fragile under post-training quantization. Grading of its 112 answers is in progress; the second adapter's replay

**Channel-wise int4 destroys E2B.** `dev-stock-rs` graded: **grounded 34/148 (23%)**, 78 not grounded, 30 unverified,
six degenerate repetition loops, two replies drifting into Spanish or Indonesian; of its 112 answers 30% were grounded
(block-32: 81%, Google: 65%). And on the Pixel 6 GPU the same bundle, and `lora-r16-2-rs` with it, give word salad on
the speed-10 prompts ("The question is answered by the direct statement of the …", 10 of 10 unverified) where Google's
bundle answers normally; the 2026-11-02 sweep runs show the same `unverified 10` and were only ever read for speed. So
the bold decline numbers above were a broken model, not a bold one.

**Where that leaves the merged route tonight.** With litert-torch 0.10.0's post-training quantization, the recipe that
keeps the model's quality (block-32 int4, 81% of answers grounded, timid) does not load on the phone GPU, and the recipe
that loads (channel-wise int4) wrecks the model. Google's bundle is quantization-aware-trained to a 2/4/8-bit mix the
converter does not offer. Two checks left: the second adapter at the quality recipe on the emulator (the by-kind question
about the adapter itself, `dev-lora-r16-2-b32` against `dev-stock-b32comp`), and one int8 export (`stock-i8`,
`dynamic_wi8_afp32`, twice the bytes) on the phone: does it load, how fast, does it make sense.

**int8 loads on the phone but is slow and still degraded.** `stock-i8` (`dynamic_wi8_afp32`, 5.07 GB bundle): loads on
the Pixel 6 GPU (2,477 MB graphics, 132 s load), decodes 7.2 tok/s, 11.6 s for 64 tokens (Google 6.9). Its speed-10
replies are coherent sentences but 6 of 10 end unverified and several read passage headers into the answer or wander
("The capital of the country is the source of the novel…"), where Google's bundle answers all 10 cleanly. So none of the
three post-training recipes gives a phone-usable E2B: channel-wise int4 wrecks it, block-32 int4 keeps it but will not
load on the GPU, int8 loads at half Google's speed with visible damage. The merged route is blocked at the converter,
not at the adapter, until litert-torch exports a quantization the GPU delegate takes without this loss (or a
quantization-aware pass of our own, which is a project, not a lever).
