# Granger brand: the mission and the keeper

**Status:** Approved by Neal, 2026-11-03, and applied to the brief (§1, §2, §3, §7), the app's tagline and motto, the README, `funding.json` and the Classics colophon. The store copy in §9 waits for Plan 11. Written the same day from Neal's notes. The direction is now the keeper in the woods (§2), not the human-rights banner. Tagline: *Your door to knowledge. Always open.* Motto: *Knowledge is for all who seek it.* (both Neal, 2026-11-03). The safety rule (§6) is agreed. The draft amends the 2026-10-23 brief (`2026-10-23-brand-vision-design.md`) §1, §3 and §7. It leaves alone the name, the mark, the palette, the type and the fair deal.
**Why now:** The grant search (`../reports/Granger grant opportunities.md`, 2 Nov 2026) showed that Granger's strongest case is access to knowledge and learning for everyone, and how many of Neal's peers abroad could use it.

## 1. The change in one line

**Mission first, ownership as the means, "anywhere" as the proof, told in the keeper's voice.** The 2026-10-23 brief led with ownership, used preparedness as the proof, and kept the mission in About and grants. Ownership stays, because it's *how* the knowledge stays in reach: once it's on your phone, no network, filter or company can take it back. The reason Granger exists now comes first, but said quietly.

| | 2026-10-23 brief | This draft |
|---|---|---|
| **Why** (mission) | About and grants only | Leads the site, README, store listing, grants and the note to peers |
| **How** (ownership) | The headline | The means: downloaded, offline, no account, nothing leaves the phone |
| **Proof** | Preparedness | Three scenes where the network isn't (§4) |
| **Character** | A capable friend with a punk streak | The keeper in the woods (§2) |

"Preparedness" is retired as a brand word. It points to bunkers, and the scenes in §4 cover the same ground without the fear.

## 2. The archetype: the keeper in the woods

Neal's direction (2026-11-03): the old sage lives outside the civilisations that come and go, like Merlin in his cottage in the woods, waiting for the young Arthur to stumble in and become his pupil. Granger waits the same way for whoever needs knowledge with no gate in front of it, and gives it.

How the keeper behaves:
- **It waits.** It doesn't push, notify or recruit. It's simply there when someone comes looking, at any hour, with no signal.
- **It sits outside the city.** It doesn't depend on the network, the store or the state, so their seasons don't touch it. This is the brief's punk streak, turned quiet: it doesn't argue with the empire, it just doesn't need it.
- **It teaches more than it tells.** The librarian shows the passage it read, says when the library doesn't know, and points the reader to the next page. The pupil leaves knowing more, not just holding an answer.
- **It's old and patient.** It's dry and warm and never in a hurry. It never announces itself.

The archetype fits stories the brand already tells. The barn owl in the gable keeps watch over the grain at night (brief §2). The book people of *Fahrenheit 451* live outside the city and keep the books until someone needs them. The granary is filled in good seasons for the lean ones.

**Two rules from the brief still hold, and the archetype lives inside them:**
- **No wizarding imagery and no Merlin by name** in public copy. Granger plus an owl plus a wizard brings Hogwarts to mind at once, which brief §2 already guards against. The keeper shows in the voice and the behaviour, never as robes, spells, stars or a beard. "Keeper", "librarian" and "old friend" are the words to use; "wizard" and "mage" aren't.
- **The owl is a mark, not the keeper's face.** The librarian has the keeper's character. The owl doesn't talk or act as its avatar (brief §8).

**Say the long view as seasons, not collapse.** "Civilisations come and go" is the internal picture. In public copy it's networks, storms and seasons that come and go, because the brief retired fear-first framing along with "Civilization, backed up."

## 3. The tagline and the motto (open)

Neal: "The world's knowledge. Yours to keep." and "Knowledge is a human right." are accurate, but they grandstand. The keeper doesn't announce rights; he leaves the door open.

**Tagline** (under the lockup, on Welcome, in About):

| Option | Why | Risk |
|---|---|---|
| **Your door to knowledge. Always open.** (recommended, 2026-11-03) | Neal's "doorway to knowledge, whenever you need it", made short. The first beat says what Granger is, with "your" for ownership; the second is the promise (no closing hours, no signal needed, open source). It stacks as two lines on Welcome | "Door/gateway to knowledge" is common on schools and libraries; search before it goes in the lockup |
| **The door is always open.** | The keeper speaking: an invitation, not a slogan | Doesn't say "knowledge"; leans on the motto |

**Motto** (opens About, the site, grants; replaces "Knowledge is a human right."). **Chosen 2026-11-03: *Knowledge is for all who seek it.*** (Neal's own line, tightened from "for anyone who seeks it"; Spanish *El conocimiento es para todos los que lo buscan.*). The options weighed before it:

| Option | Why |
|---|---|
| **For whoever comes looking.** (recommended) | The keeper waiting at the door, open to anyone. It claims nothing grand, and "looking" is what a reader does |
| **Kept for anyone who asks.** | Ties to Ask and the librarian |

**The pair:** *Your door to knowledge. Always open.* under the lockup, and *Knowledge is for all who seek it.* opening About.

**Mission statement** (for the README, store, grants and pitches; written in the keeper's voice):

> *Granger keeps the world's knowledge, and a librarian you can ask, on a phone you own. It waits, with no signal, for whoever needs it: in the air, off the grid, or wherever the network doesn't reach or isn't allowed to.*

"Isn't allowed to" covers censorship without naming a government, which suits both the keeper's quiet and the safety rule (§6). It answers the earlier question of "a government's filter" versus a softer phrase.

## 4. Three scenes where the network isn't

These are the use cases, in the order the brand tells them: everyday first, hardest last. Every claim in the "today" column is true of the current build. Everything under "planned" stays out of public copy until it ships (brief §7: "Every claim is literal").

| Scene | Who | What Granger does today | Planned, not claimed yet |
|---|---|---|---|
| **In the air, on the road** | Travellers, commuters, students with no data plan or roaming | Wikipedia, reference works, books and audiobooks on the phone; the librarian answers from them and cites the passage; read-aloud voices | — |
| **Off the grid** | Campers, sailors, rural households, field workers, anyone after a storm | Medicine and Survival a tap from Home; a map of your region; reading aloud when your eyes are busy; no server to wait on | Phones with 3–4 GB of memory (E1); smaller packs |
| **Where the network isn't allowed** | Students, teachers, nurses and families where sites are blocked, data is throttled or the internet is cut | What's on the phone can't be filtered; the librarian runs on the phone, so no question is logged anywhere; no telemetry; the catalog is signed | Phone-to-phone and SD-card sharing with tamper checks (PS); Ask in Spanish; Swahili (SW); a threat model and audit |

**Learning runs through all three scenes.** In each one there's a pupil at the door: a student, a teacher or a curious reader. Learning is what joins the scenes, so it doesn't get a fourth row. It's also the keeper's whole purpose, and what the education funders (Tools, Solve, a Gemma prize) want to see.

**Cost is the quiet fourth case.** Many people have a phone and some signal but can't pay for the data. Mention it in one clause, "or can't afford the data", never as a scene of its own. A "digital divide" pitch is ICT4D, which OTF rules out, and it talks down to the reader.

## 6. The safety rule (agreed 2026-11-03): the mission is said outside, the app is quiet

Where the state watches what people carry, a phone can be searched. An app that calls itself a censorship tool marks its owner. The keeper never announces himself either.

1. **Inside the app it's a library.** The name, icon, Welcome, notifications and Home never use words like censorship, resist, freedom, banned or regime. Today's in-app copy already passes; keep it that way.
2. **The mission is said where the reader chose to look:** the website, the README, grants and press. The store listing says "outages and disruptions" (Neal, 2026-11-03).
3. **No named governments in product copy.** Grants and the threat model name countries because the funder must know (§7).
4. **Testers stay unnamed.** Peers who test are described by role and region, never by name or photo, in any channel.

## 7. Naming countries: what the field does

The internet-freedom field splits countries into two kinds of fact, and treats them differently.

- **Censorship itself is public record, so name it.** OONI, Access Now and NetBlocks name countries and dates for every block and shutdown. Citing their reports by country, as context, puts no one at risk.
- **Where a tool is used or tested is about people, so protect it.** Tor and Psiphon publish usage by country, but only as large aggregates that point to no one. Projects working with small groups describe them by region and role and keep partners and pilot sites out of public materials. They get informed consent from testers before saying anything, and they let the testers decide. A shared rule of thumb is "do no harm" (the responsible-data approach): don't publish what you can't take back if it puts someone in danger.

Granger's case is the second kind, and the numbers are small. Twenty or thirty testers in one country, recruited by one developer through his own contacts, can be traced back to those contacts. The draft's rule:

1. **Website and README:** regions only ("readers in the Caribbean, South America and East Africa"). Cite public censorship reports by country only as context, never next to "we test there".
2. **Grants:** name the countries, because funders need them. Ask OTF whether its public summary of a funded project would name them, and ask for regions there if so.
3. **Usage numbers by country:** only once a country has enough installs that the number points to no group. A threshold of a few thousand is a reasonable start; confirm it in the threat model (plan item 3.1).
4. **Ask the testers.** Their consent, and their sense of the local risk, overrides all of the above.

This is the general practice as described here, not a sourced policy. Check it with OTF and with the contacts themselves before testing starts.

## 8. Voice: additions to brief §7

- **The attitude (revised):** the keeper in the woods (§2). He's self-reliant, patient and dry. The brief's defiance stays, but quiet: Granger doesn't need the network's permission, and doesn't make a speech about it. It aims at the filter and the tether, never at a person, a people or a country.
- **Say (add):** keep, kept, wait, open, learn, ask, whoever, look it up, nothing leaves the phone.
- **Avoid (add):**
    - **Grandstanding:** human right, revolution, change the world, democratise knowledge.
    - **Overclaims:** censorship-proof, uncensorable, can't be stopped, unblockable. A download can still be blocked until hand-to-hand sharing ships. Say "once it's on your phone, no filter reaches it" instead.
    - **Development language:** bridging the digital divide, the underserved, the developing world, empower.
    - **Battle language:** fight, liberate, weapon, resistance.
    - **Wizard words:** magic, spell, wizard, enchanted (§2).
- **Sounds like:** "Networks come and go. The library stays." · "Ask it anything in your library. Nobody else hears the question." · "Wikipedia at 35,000 feet." · "It'll be here when you need it." · "No gate. No fee. No one watching."

I'm looking for people who'd use it where the connection is unreliable, expensive or watched, and who'll tell me honestly what's missing. Want to try it?
