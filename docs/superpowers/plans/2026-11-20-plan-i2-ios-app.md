# Plan I2: The iPhone app and online reading — dev-friend build 1

> **For agentic workers:** REQUIRED SUB-SKILL: Use superpowers:subagent-driven-development (recommended) or superpowers:executing-plans to implement this plan task-by-task. Steps use checkbox (`- [ ]`) syntax for tracking.

**Goal:** A real Granger app on the iPhone that a developer friend can clone, open in Xcode and install. It reads Wikipedia online the way the Android app does: you search, land on an article's topic page, read the full article in Granger's own look, and follow its links. Home has today's featured article and what you read lately. Settings has the Wikipedia language, appearance, text size and Local only. Nothing is kept offline yet: that's I3.

**Spec:** `docs/superpowers/specs/2026-11-20-iphone-design.md` §2–§6 (I2). **Builds on:** I1 (`2026-11-20-plan-i1-shared-core.md`): `GrangerCore` (shared Kotlin over SKIE) and `ios/GrangerCore` (the Swift package). **Roadmap:** the I1+ row.

**Architecture:**
- **One SwiftUI app, `ios/Granger`**, generated by XcodeGen from `ios/Granger/project.yml` and committed (spec §4). It replaces `ios/CoreSmoke`. Bundle ID `app.granger` (spec §9), signing from `ios/Local.xcconfig`, iOS 26.
- **The core does the reading; Swift draws.** Wikipedia comes through `:core:online` (`WikimediaClient`, `OnlineWikipediaArchive`) exactly as on Android. What iOS adds is the web host and the screens. Small `@Observable` models call the core off the main thread (its calls block).
- **The same page as Android.** Articles render in a `WKWebView` from the core's transformed HTML with the same `online.css` and the same page scripts. Those move from `:app` into the core (Task 2), so both phones load one copy. A `WKURLSchemeHandler` for a `granger-zim:` scheme does what Android's `ZimWebViewClient` does: it serves the page, its pictures (`_img/…`) and the stylesheet from the archive, and it refuses everything else. A short shim gives the scripts the `GrangerBridge` object they call on Android, posting to `webkit.messageHandlers`.
- **Tabs (spec §5):** **Home** and **Search** in I2; **Explore** (I3) and **Library** (I4/I5) appear when they're built rather than as empty tabs. Settings opens from Home's header. Articles and topic pages are pushed on the tab's own navigation stack, so swipe-back works.

**Tech stack:** Swift 6, SwiftUI (iOS 26), WebKit (`WKWebView`, `WKURLSchemeHandler`, `WKScriptMessageHandler`), SafariServices for outside links, XcodeGen, `GrangerCore` 2026-11-20 or a newer release cut in Task 2, XCTest/XCUITest on the iOS 27.1 Simulator.

## Builder's calls in this plan (for Neal's review)

1. **Two tabs in I2 (Home, Search), not four with placeholders.** An empty "coming soon" tab tells a tester nothing; the tab bar grows as Explore (I3) and Library (I4/I5) land.
2. **Home shows today's featured article, not Wander's random article.** Android's Wander draws from the well-known articles in the downloaded index and filters adult topics by its flags (Plan AF). I2 has no index, and Wikipedia's own random can land anywhere. So Home shows Wikipedia's **featured article of the day** (curated by its editors; `/feed/featured`'s `tfa`, a new `WikimediaClient.featured` call in the core), plus **Recently read**. Checked 2026-11-20: the English feed has a featured article and the Spanish one doesn't, but both carry Wikimedia's curated **Picture of the day** (`image`), so a Spanish Home shows that instead. Wander comes in I3 with the index.
3. **No Related on the topic page in I2.** Android's online Related (`morelike:`) is filtered against the index's adult flags (Plan AF); without the index the iPhone can't filter it. The I2 topic page has the picture, the summary, **Read the full article** and **Read in English/Español** (from the article's language links). Related and the records come in I3.
4. **English app text only in I2**, in a String Catalog with the same words as Android's `strings*.xml` (copied, not invented). Spanish for the app's own text comes in I6, generated from Android's Spanish strings. Wikipedia itself can be English or Spanish from the start.
5. **The page scripts and `online.css` move into the core** (`:core:online` resources as Kotlin string constants) and Android reads them from there. That's one copy for both phones. Android's behaviour doesn't change, and its tests prove it.
6. **A `granger-zim:` scheme on iOS.** `WKWebView` can't intercept `https`, so iOS pages load from `granger-zim://zim/<pack>/<path>`. `ZimUrls` gains the base as a setting; Android keeps `https://zim.granger.invalid/`.
7. **The app icon is traced from the Android launcher's owl mark** (`app/src/main/res/drawable/ic_launcher_foreground.xml`, rendered at 1024 px on the Field background), not redrawn ("Marks: trace, don't draw"). Neal checks it in Task 1.
8. **Checks are on the Simulator.** Neal has no iPhone at hand, so the walkthrough runs on the iOS 27.1 Simulator with screenshots and an XCUITest smoke test. A dev friend's first install on a real iPhone is the device check, and their notes come back as issues or email.

## Global Constraints

- **Android must not change for the user.** Task 2 is the only task touching shared or Android code; it ends with the whole JVM suite passing and a debug build on the Pixel 6, opening an online article and a pack article as before (shared-phone rules: check `lastUpdateTime` and the DB version first; `adb -s` the Pixel's serial).
- **The repo lives on the X10** (`~/Developer/Granger` → `/Volumes/X10 Pro/Developer/Granger`); it must be mounted. Builds go to the worktree's `build/` and Xcode's DerivedData in the scratchpad. The Mac's free space is watched; nothing big goes in `~`.
- **The Mac is shared.** One Gradle or Kotlin/Native build at a time; `-Pkotlin.compiler.execution.strategy=in-process`.
- **Wikimedia etiquette:** Granger's User-Agent (`GrangerCoreEntry.userAgent`), the client's 4-per-host limit, and nothing fetched before the privacy line is seen (Android O5's rule).
- **No telemetry.** Nothing leaves the phone except requests to Wikimedia.
- **Pushing, GitHub releases and merges need Neal's OK.** Before any push: `git log --format=%ae origin/main..HEAD | sort -u` shows only the noreply address.

### Task 2: The shared page pieces (the only task touching Android)

**Files:** `core/online/src/commonMain/…/ReaderPage.kt` (new: `ONLINE_CSS`, `PAGE_SCRIPT`, the contents/heading helper), `core/zim-api/…/ZimUrls.kt`, `core/online/…/WikimediaClient.kt` (`featured`, `languageLinks`), `shared/…/GrangerCoreEntry.kt`; Android: `app/src/main/assets/reader/online.css` → read from the core, `ReaderPageScript.SOURCE` → from the core, `ZimWebViewClient`.

- [ ] Move `online.css` and `ReaderPageScript.SOURCE` (the tap/link script) into `:core:online` as constants; Android's `ZimWebViewClient` and `ReaderWebView` read them from there (the asset file goes). The read-aloud script stays on Android until I5.
- [ ] `ZimUrls`: the base (`https://zim.granger.invalid/`) becomes settable once at start (`ZimUrls.base`); iOS sets `granger-zim://zim/`. The transform's stylesheet link follows it. Tests for both bases (encode, parse, fragment).
- [ ] `WikimediaClient.featured(lang, date)`: today's featured article (`/api/rest_v1/feed/featured/YYYY/MM/DD`, its `tfa`) as a `Summary`, and the Picture of the day (`image`: thumbnail, description, Commons page) for wikis whose feed has no `tfa` (Spanish, checked 2026-11-20). `languageLinks(lang, title, to)`: the other language's title (`action=query&prop=langlinks`). Parser tests from saved JSON on both platforms.
- [ ] `GrangerCoreEntry` additions: `setZimBase`, `onlineSources(version, allowed: () -> Bool)` giving the client with a gate (nothing fetched until the privacy line is seen, nothing in Local only), `pageScript`, `onlineCss`, `headings(html)` (ids and titles of h2/h3, through `ArticleText`).
- [ ] Android unchanged: whole JVM suite passes; debug build on the Pixel 6 opens an online article (styled, pictures, links) and a pack article. Cut a new `GrangerCore` release (`tools/ios/release-core.sh --publish`) with Neal's OK; pin it. Commit.

### Task 3: The web host

**Files:** `ios/Granger/Sources/Reader/ArticleWebView.swift`, `ZimSchemeHandler.swift`, `PageBridge.swift`.

- [ ] `ZimSchemeHandler` (`WKURLSchemeHandler`, scheme `granger-zim`): parses the URL with the core's `ZimUrls`, reads the entry from the right archive off the main thread (`OnlineWikipediaArchive.read`), answers with its MIME type and bytes; `_granger/online.css` from the core's constant; anything else 404. Cancelled tasks are dropped cleanly (a fast swipe-back mustn't crash).
- [ ] `ArticleWebView` (`UIViewRepresentable`): one `WKWebView` with the scheme handler, the shim (`window.GrangerBridge = { noteLink, toggleChrome, … }` posting to `webkit.messageHandlers.granger`) and the core's page script injected at document end; `color-scheme` follows the app's theme; text size through `-webkit-text-size-adjust` from the setting.
- [ ] Links (Android's `linkAction` rules, same outcomes): an article link in the same wiki pushes that article's topic page; a `#fragment` scrolls; an outside link opens in `SFSafariViewController`; `mailto:`/`tel:` go to the system; `javascript:`, `file:` and anything unknown are ignored.
- [ ] XCTest for the scheme handler's routing (page, picture, stylesheet, unknown, cancelled) with a fake archive. Commit.
